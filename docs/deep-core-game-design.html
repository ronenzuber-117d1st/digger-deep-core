Deep Core – Game Design Doc

Working title · roguelite remake of Digger · pixel art · browser first · solo project · v0.1

0. Assumptions and changes vs. the plan

Decisions locked (your defaults):
  • Combat: gold bags stay the signature weapon, plus one auto-firing drill pulse that upgrades during a run.
  • Death: keep 50% of emeralds. Extraction keeps 100%.
  • Art: 16×16 tiles, 32-colour palette, one shared palette file.
  • Success: a friend finishes a first dive unaided and starts a second one voluntarily.
  • Crew: engineer, cartographer, mysterious radio voice.
  • Name: working title "Deep Core"; final name before release.
Assumptions I added (veto any):
  • Single-screen floors (30×17 tiles, like the original) instead of scrolling levels. It removes camera and scrolling risk. The camera is used only for shake and zoom.
  • Original monsters, names and art. Behaviours are inspired by the classic, but nothing is copied from it.
  • Light unit tests for the game logic (grid, bag physics, economy). No formal QA tooling.
  • Keyboard first. Touch and gamepad come later via an input abstraction.

1. Product goal

Ship a polished, replayable browser roguelite in about 5 weeks (7 with buffer), in which a 6 to 12 minute dive is fun on its own and the upgrade loop makes players want "one more dive". There is no revenue goal. The goal is a finished, shareable game and a good vibe-coding project.

2. Target audience

  • Primary: you and friends. Players who like Vampire Survivors and Dead Cells, enjoy short sessions, and may remember classic DOS games.
  • Secondary: itch.io visitors browsing for quick pixel-art roguelites.
  • Behaviour: keyboard on desktop, sessions of 10 to 30 minutes, no tutorial patience. The first dive must teach itself.

3. Problem and value proposition

Problem

The 1983 original is fun, but it has no progression, one-hit deaths, and dated visuals. Many modern roguelites are bigger and more complex than a casual session needs.

Value

A one-screen-per-floor digging action game with a satisfying signature move (crushing monsters with gold bags), real build variety through run-time picks, and a permanent upgrade loop. Add a light story to give the dives meaning.

6. Core rules spec (v0.1, tune in playtests)

World

  • Floor: 30×17 tiles of 16 px (internal 480×272, integer-scaled). Tile types: dirt, empty, bedrock (border), plus biome tiles.
  • Player starts at the top-left open area. The exit shaft is at the bottom-right.
  • Floors per biome: 5. Total floors in a full run: 20.

Movement and digging

  • 4-direction, tile-aligned movement. Open tunnel: 6 tiles/s. Digging dirt: 4 tiles/s (upgradeable).

Fuel

  • Starts at 100 and drains 1/s. Emerald +2, canister +20 (1 to 2 per floor), clearing a floor +15.
  • At 0, you take 1 damage per second. This is a soft fail, not instant death.
  • Target dive length: 6 to 12 minutes for a typical dive.

Health

  • 3 HP. After taking damage you get 1.0 s of invulnerability.

Gold bags

  • 3 to 6 per floor, embedded in dirt. If the tile below becomes empty, the bag starts to fall.
  • Falls 1 tile: stays intact and can be pushed sideways by the player.
  • Falls 2 or more tiles, or lands on a monster: it breaks and spills 5 nuggets (3 emeralds each).
  • Any falling bag kills a non-boss monster it lands on.
  • Nuggets vanish after 8 seconds. Players must go and grab them. This is the "risk" in the risk-reward.
  • A bag landing on the player deals 1 damage.

Drill pulse

  • Fires every 1.2 s in the facing direction. Range 3 tiles, 1 damage, stops at the first target.

Death and extraction

  • Death at HP 0: keep 50% of emeralds. Extraction points appear at floors 5, 10, 15 and 20: keep 100%, and you can also choose to continue.

8. Story and crew (light)

Tessa, the engineer

Gruff and practical. Runs the upgrade bench. Short, funny lines.

Orin, the cartographer

Nervous and curious. Comments on biomes and reveals lore about what lives below.

"Static", the radio voice

Unknown. Starts to speak after floor 5, encouraging you to go deeper.
  • Premise: the mine has no recorded bottom. Something at the Core is humming, and the crew wants to know what.
  • Ending (no branching): the Heart is an ancient power source, and Static is its caretaker AI. Defeating the Heart shuts it down. Static thanks you, and the crew celebrates. A "dive again" option stays unlocked.
  • Delivery: about 15 short scenes of 1 to 6 lines, triggered by firstDeath, reachedFloor(N), afterBoss, and returnToBase(count).

9. Player flow

  1. Title → Base (permanent upgrades, crew scenes, Dive button).
  2. Dive: floor 1 → dig and fight → reach the exit shaft → next floor. Every 2nd floor shows the run-pick screen.
  3. Extraction point (floors 5, 10, 15, 20): choose to extract or continue.
  4. Death: summary (floors, emeralds banked, kills) → Base.
  5. Error and edge states: save missing or corrupt → start a fresh save and show a notice. Tab hidden → auto-pause.

10. Art, audio and UX

  • Resolution: 480×272 internal, integer scaled, nearest-neighbour. Tiles and characters 16×16, bosses 48×48.
  • Style rules: max 32 colours from one shared palette (below). No anti-aliasing and no gradients. One light direction (top-left). Outlines in a dark palette colour.
  • Palette: the 32 colours below are the single source of truth. Every generated asset gets quantised to this palette before it enters the project.
  • Audio: jsfxr for effects. Two music loops (base and dive), plus one for bosses. All must have a clear license.
  • Accessibility: rebindable keys, a screen shake toggle, colour-blind-safe monster and hazard shapes (not colour alone), and independent music and SFX volume.

11. Technical requirements

  • Stack: Phaser 3, TypeScript, Vite, Vitest. Static hosting (itch.io or GitHub Pages).
  • Architecture: a pure-TypeScript core/ (grid, entities, rules, RNG, economy) with no Phaser imports, fixed 60 Hz update. A render/ layer that reads the state and draws. scenes/ for UI flow. data/ for JSON (upgrades, monsters, biomes, dialogue).
  • Determinism: seeded RNG (mulberry32). The same seed produces the same floor. Seed shown on the summary screen.
  • Input: an abstraction layer (Action enum). Keyboard: arrows/WASD, Space (bomb), Esc (pause). Touch and gamepad later.
  • Performance targets: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop. Bundle under 5 MB. Load time under 3 s.
  • Debug panel (from day 1): FPS, depth skip, god mode, spawn control, seed set, add emeralds.
  • Platform: desktop Chrome/Firefox/Edge. Mobile later via Capacitor.

12. Data model

interface SaveData {
  version: number;              // for migrations
  emeralds: number;             // banked currency
  upgrades: Record<PermanentUpgradeId, number>;  // level per upgrade
  storyFlags: string[];         // seen scene ids
  stats: { runs: number; deaths: number; bestFloor: number; bossesKilled: string[] };
  settings: { musicVol: number; sfxVol: number; shake: boolean; keys: Record<Action, string> };
}
interface RunState {
  seed: number; floor: number; hp: number; maxHp: number;
  fuel: number; emeraldsCarried: number;
  picks: RunUpgradeId[];        // chosen run-time upgrades
  rngState: number;
}
interface FloorState {
  tiles: TileType[][];          // 30 x 17
  entities: Entity[];           // player, monsters, bags, nuggets, pickups, nests
  biome: BiomeId;
}
interface UpgradeDef { id: string; kind: 'run' | 'permanent'; maxLevel: number; baseCost?: number; effect: Effect[]; }
interface MonsterDef { id: string; hp: number; speed: number; behaviour: string; minFloor: number; }
interface BiomeDef  { id: string; floors: [number, number]; hazards: string[]; monsterPool: string[]; boss?: string; }
interface DialogueScene { id: string; trigger: Trigger; lines: { speaker: string; text: string }[]; }
  • Storage: localStorage key deepcore.save.v1, always wrapped in try/catch, with a version field and a migration function. Export/import of the save as a JSON file is a nice-to-have.

13. Light test strategy

  • Unit tests (Vitest) on core/: bag fall, push and break rules; fuel drain; economy and cost formula; seed determinism; save migration.
  • Playtest checklist per week: a full dive without crashes, no soft-locks (can always reach the exit), the fuel timer is fair, the first dive is clearable.
  • Soft-lock guard: the generator must guarantee that the exit and at least one fuel canister can be reached by digging.

Scope creep (biomes, bosses, story)HighAnything outside the MVP list waits until after the week 3 milestone.
AI art style driftHighLock the palette now, quantise every asset, reject off-style sprites.
Week 3 overload (2 biomes + picks + art)MediumMove one biome or the art pass into week 4 if week 2 slips past day 10.
Balance takes longer than codingHighData-driven JSON for numbers, and a debug panel to tweak quickly.
Soft-locks in generated floorsMediumReachability check in the generator, plus unit tests.
IP exposure from the "Digger" name and original assetsMediumOriginal art and sound only, and a distinct final title.
